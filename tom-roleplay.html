How to roleplay Tom Bin

A Man of Bree, a Treasure Hunter, and a would-be map-maker chasing his runaway donkey. A guideline, not a rulebook. Works offline. Prints on paper.

This page gives ideas for how Tom talks, acts and feels. Roleplay has no wrong answers. Take what is fun and drop the rest. Numbers like p.40 are printed pages in the core rulebook, The One Ring 2nd edition. Book facts are short summaries in my own words.

Source labels (read this first)

  • Book comes from the core rulebook. The page number is next to it. Each one was checked in the book.
  • Lore is Middle-earth knowledge from Tolkien's books, from memory. It was not checked against the core book. Ask your Loremaster before you rely on it.
  • Our idea is made for Tom by his player and this kit. It is in no book. Change it freely.

Game words: the Loremaster is the person who runs the game (the game master) p.8. A Culture is your hero's people. A Calling is what drives your hero to adventure, like a class in other games p.24. A TN (Target Number) is the number a roll must reach p.18.

1. Tom in one look

Our idea Tom Bin is 21, a plain farm lad from a village in the Bree-land. He is friendly, a bit clumsy, and good with animals. He calls himself a cartographer (a map-maker), but he never learned how. So he asks people, he guesses, and he draws a homemade map of every place he has already searched. He talks easily. He tells tall tales just as easily.

Our idea A year ago, a storm scared off his donkey, Tin. Tin has a silvery, shiny coat (that is where the name comes from) and a black mask around his eyes, like a raccoon. Tom has followed every rumour of "the masked donkey" since. Tin is always one step ahead. The trail has led Tom south, to the hall of a small Dunland tribe at the foot of the Misty Mountains.

Strength 4 (TN 16)Heart 4 (TN 16)Wits 6 (TN 13)Endurance 24Hope 14Parry 18 with buckler

What he wants

Our idea To catch Tin, go home with a full purse, and never be laughed at again.

What he fears

Our idea That someone else gets to Tin before he does. And that one day, his stories stop being believed.

His heart

Our idea Warm, honest with animals, less honest with people. He means well, mostly. Treasure makes his eyes shine a little too much.

Three lines to start with

"Tom Bin, cartographer. Well, I make maps. Well, I make a map. Have you seen a donkey with a mask?"

"Don't mind me. I'm just marking where I've already looked."

"That donkey is all I have left of my old gran. Or my mother. It's a long story. It's a sad one, I promise."

2. His Culture: Men of Bree

A short summary of Tom's people, and how others see him.

The Bree-folk in short

  • Book Their families have been in the Bree-land since the earliest days of Men in Eriador. Big roads meet at Bree, so news and stories from far away pass through its inn p.40.
  • Book Picture sturdy, cheerful country folk. Easy-going on the outside, but nobody's servant and nobody's fool p.40.
  • Book Few Bree-folk ever go adventuring. The few who do set out young and are expected home, settled, before middle age p.40. Tom, at 21, fits this well.
  • Book Bree is a peaceful place. Its people fight with the kind of gear a farmer or a hunter already knows p.40. Tom's spear and bow fit this.
  • Book Their own old language is gone. They speak the Common Tongue with a Bree flavour, and their names are short and homely, much like Hobbit names. "Tom" is a typical Bree name p.41.
  • Book Bree is the biggest of four villages; the others are Staddle, Combe and Archet. A hedge and a ditch ring the town. Men and Hobbits share it and get on well, and everyone loves a traveller's tale p.180 p.181.

His Cultural Blessing: Bree-blood

Book Bree-folk are used to strangers of every kind, so Tom mixes well with other peoples. The rule: with Tom in the group, the Company's Fellowship pool is 1 point bigger p.40. (The Company is your group of heroes. Fellowship is a shared pool of points the group spends to get Hope back while resting p.54 p.71.)

Our idea Play this as Tom being easy to like. He shares his pipe, asks after people's families, and remembers names. He is the one who says hello first.

"An Elf! At my fire! Wait till they hear this at home. Sit, sit. Mind the map."

Sayings and customs from home

  • Book One of the Bree Virtues is named after an old saying, "Strange as News from Bree" p.88. Tom can use it about anything odd: "Well, that's strange as news from Bree."
  • Book Pipe-weed is closely tied to Bree; there is even a Bree Virtue about smoking p.88. A pipe at the fire suits Tom, even without that Virtue.
  • Book Bree's famous inn is the Prancing Pony p.181. Tom compares every hall, ale and bed to "the Pony", and they always lose.
  • Our idea Homely words, not an accent: "reckon", "aye", "fair enough", "bless me", "that's a rum thing". One or two per scene is plenty.

How others see him

  • Our idea In the Dunland hall, he is a foreigner from the north: harmless, a bit comic, and useful. He chops wood, fixes things, and talks. The tribe may find his stories funny, or start to doubt them.
  • Lore In Tolkien's books, Dunland lies in the west, below the southern end of the Misty Mountains. Its people, the Dunlendings, have old grudges against the Men of Rohan. From memory, not checked. The core rulebook names Dunland only once and gives no rules for it p.219.
  • Lore Tolkien's appendices hint that the Men of Bree and the Dunlendings may share very old roots. From memory, not checked. If your Loremaster likes it, it is a lovely hook: "My gran said our folk came from the south, long ago."
  • Our idea To Elves and Dwarves, he is a friendly country lad who asks too many questions.

3. His Calling and his Shadow Path

Treasure Hunter

Book A Treasure Hunter goes after the lost riches of old kingdoms, even into very dangerous places p.45. They get two Favoured skills from Explore, Scan and Stealth. Tom picked Scan and Stealth p.45. (A Favoured skill rolls two Feat dice and keeps the better one p.20.)

Book The Calling also gives the Distinctive Feature Burglary: a thief's knack, such as light fingers, locks, and getting into places that are shut p.45.

Our idea Tom does not think of himself as a treasure hunter. He thinks of himself as a donkey hunter who keeps finding interesting things on the way. His "treasure" talk is about maps: "Every map has a spot nobody has drawn yet."

"I'm not saying it's a hidden door. I'm saying I've drawn this wall three times, and it's shorter on the outside."

His Distinctive Features: Rustic, Inquisitive, Burglary

Book These three words sum up Tom. Let them guide what he does p.67.

  • Book Rustic: plain country manners; fancy show does not impress him p.68.
  • Book Inquisitive: nosy, and that nosiness makes him hard to trick p.68.
  • Book Burglary: see above p.45.

Book Why they matter in play: before a skill roll, you can name a feature that plausibly helps. Then Tom is Inspired: if he spends 1 Hope on that roll, he gets 2 bonus Success dice instead of 1 p.67 p.20. So describe Tom through his features. It is good roleplay, and it is good rules.

"I'm nosy, I know. Let me look at that letter. Just the seal. Just a peek." (Inquisitive, on an Insight or Scan roll)

Shadow Path: Dragon-sickness

Book In one word: greed. The longer Tom hunts treasure, the more he may care about owning things, and the less about people p.46.

Book How Shadow comes: touching tainted treasure from an old hoard can give Shadow points through Greed. Tom can resist it with a Wisdom roll against his Wits TN 13 p.138 p.29. His Wisdom is 1, so that is 1 Success die, or 2 if he spends Hope p.20.

Book Some wrong acts (Misdeeds) give Shadow, and no roll can stop it. A lie told to harm someone is a small one, 1 point. Stealing is a big one, 3 points p.138 p.139. The Loremaster should warn you first p.138.

Book When Shadow reaches his maximum Hope (14), he has a bout of madness: a short loss of control that clears his Shadow but moves him one step down his Shadow Path p.139. Each step gives him a Flaw, a bad trait. The first one he would meet is Grasping: wanting riches just to have them. Later steps get darker, towards suspicion, lies and theft p.140 p.141. A Flaw makes fitting rolls Ill-favoured p.141. (Ill-favoured means two Feat dice, keep the worse p.20.)

Play it as a temptation, not a punishment. Our idea The fun is in the pull. Let the table see Tom want something, and let him sometimes win against it. A Shadow point is a story beat, not a failure.

Our idea Small ways to show the pull:

  • He counts the shared coins twice, then pushes one more to his friend. "Fair's fair."
  • He picks up a shiny thing "for luck" and holds it a moment too long.
  • When treasure is near, his stories get shorter and his eyes stay on the chest.
  • When he resists, say it out loud: "No. Tin first. Always Tin first."

"Lovely, that. Somebody should keep it safe. I'm somebody."

"...No. You hold it. I'll only stare at it."

4. Habits, hooks and small details

Things Tom does that cost no rules and make him memorable.

His homemade maps

Book A useful item linked to a skill gives 1 bonus Success die on a fitting roll outside combat, if the Loremaster agrees p.49. Tom's maps are linked to Explore.

Our idea They are scraps of hide and bark, tied with string. He marks searched places with a cross. Every map has a tiny donkey drawn in a corner. He asks strangers to "add a bit" to his map.

"Could you draw your valley for me? Just roughly. No, the river goes the other way. Ah. You're right."

The wind-proof lantern

Book A wind-proof lantern linked to Scan is one of the book's own examples of a useful item p.50.

Our idea It is his pride. He polishes it while he talks. He holds it up at every dark doorway, even in daylight, out of habit.

"Storms, wind, a sneeze from a troll: this lantern stays lit. Best thing I own. After Tin."

Tin, the missing donkey

Our idea Tom asks everyone about a donkey with a mask. He carries a dried apple "for when I find him". He has a special whistle he swears Tin knows. Every rumour of a masked donkey makes him stand up mid-sentence.

Book A mount lowers your Fatigue at the end of a journey by its Vigour rating p.50 p.111. While Tin is lost, Tom has no help there. Ask your Loremaster what Tin's Vigour is when he comes back.

His sad stories

Our idea Tom tells sad stories about why the donkey matters so much. They rarely match. Pick one each time:

  1. "He was Grandad's last gift, the day before he died."
  2. "He was my late mother's. She raised him from a foal."
  3. "He pulled me out of the river when I was a boy."
  4. "I promised him to my sweetheart. No donkey, no wedding."

Our idea The joke works best when another player notices: "Last week it was your mother." Tom: "...Both. It was both of them."

Tall tales and the Shadow. Book Malicious lies count as a Misdeed and give 1 Shadow point p.138 p.139. Our idea Harmless tall tales for sympathy are flavour. A lie told to cheat or hurt someone is a different thing. If you are not sure, ask your Loremaster before Tom says it.

Five table moments

The map check

Set-up: the group reaches a new place.

"Hold on." (He unrolls a scrap, licks a charcoal stick, draws.) "There. Now it exists."

Ten seconds. It reminds the table he is a map-maker.

The donkey whistle

Set-up: a quiet moment outdoors.

(Two short notes, one long.) "...Worth a try. He always comes. Eventually. Sometimes."

The Loremaster may answer with a far-off bray. Or not.

The wrong story

Set-up: someone asks why the donkey matters.

"He was my grandad's. ...My mother's grandad's. It's complicated."

Fun when a friend at the table remembers last week's version.

The shiny thing

Set-up: the group finds treasure.

(Picks up a ring. Stares. Puts it down. Picks it up.) "I'll just carry it. For safety."

This shows the dragon-sickness without any rules.

The kind lie

Set-up: a scared child, or a sad friend.

"Don't worry. I've been lost loads of times. Look, here's the map. We're right here. Probably."

Shows his warm side. A good moment for Enhearten 2 if a roll is needed.

6. Keep it fun, not annoying

Not this
Do this
A Bree accent all evening.
One homely word per scene: "reckon", "aye", "bless me".
A new sad story every five minutes.
One story per session, when someone asks.
Long lectures about maps.
One line, then show the map.
Taking a friend's share of treasure.
Wanting it, out loud, then sharing it. The temptation is the scene.
Lying to the Company to cheat them.
Tall tales for strangers. With friends, he is clumsy but loyal.
Stopping the game to hunt for Tin.
Ask about Tin in every place, then follow the group's plan.
Pretending to be brave.
Being scared and staying anyway. That is a Bree hero.

Easy check: if your friends at the table are smiling, keep going. If they look at the clock, shorten it. You never need a voice. Saying "Tom polishes his lantern and asks about the donkey" is real roleplay.
