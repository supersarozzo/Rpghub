Tom Bin combos: "if this happens, do this"

Eight situations for your Man of Bree Treasure Hunter, one card each, with his exact numbers. Works offline. Prints on paper.

Numbers like p.96 are printed pages in the core rulebook, The One Ring 2nd edition. Rules are short summaries in my own words. Chances like about 50% are my own count over every dice result; they are not in the book. If the Loremaster rules differently, the Loremaster wins.

1. A fight is coming

When: the Loremaster says a fight is about to start, and there is still some distance between the two sides.

  1. Opening volley. Each side usually gets at least one shot before the melee p.94. Shoot the bow: Feat die + 1 Success die against TN 16 + the enemy's Parry. An enemy's Parry is a number like +1 or +2 added to your TN p.98 p.144. A hit costs the enemy 3 Endurance.
  2. Or throw the spear. The spear can be thrown p.48 p.94. It rolls 2 Success dice and does 4 damage, but then it is gone. Picking it up again costs a main action p.97.
  3. Raise the buckler. If you see the shots coming, your shield bonus doubles during the volleys: Parry 20 instead of 18 p.94.
  4. Pick a stance for round 1 p.95 p.96:

ForwardYour attacks +1d. Attacks on you +1d too.Intimidate Foe uses Awe 0. Weak for Tom p.102.
OpenNo change either way.Rally Comrades uses Enhearten 2, TN 16 p.102.

Why: Tom is a light fighter, not a warrior. The volley is a free try, and the buckler makes him harder to hit while the enemy closes in. His real choice is the stance.

Example: Four Orc soldiers (Parry +1, so TN 17 p.150) come out of the trees. Tom shoots: Feat 9 + Success 5 = 14. Miss. Arrows fly back at him, but his Parry is 20 for the volley. Next round he picks Rearward (card 2).

2. Stay back and shoot

When: your friends can hold the front line. Rearward is allowed only if the enemies are no more than twice the number of heroes, and only if two other heroes fight in close combat for each hero in Rearward p.96.

  1. Choose Rearward. You use the bow only, and only enemy shooters can target you p.96.
  2. Main action: Prepare Shot. Roll Scan: Scan 3, Favoured, against Wits TN 13 about 90%. On a success your next bow shot gets +1d, plus 1d more for each icon p.103.
  3. Next round: shoot. Feat die + 1 Success die + the bonus, against TN 16 + Parry. Add Hope for one more die p.20.
  4. If you hit and roll an icon: spend it on Pierce. A bow adds +2 to the Feat die number, so a Feat die of 8 or more becomes a Piercing Blow (normally only 10 or the rune) p.99. The enemy must then roll to avoid a Wound against Injury 14 p.99.
Prepare Shot about 90%shot +1d about 21% vs TN 17+1 Hope: about 46%

Why: Tom's bow is weak (rank 1), but his Scan is excellent. Preparing turns one bad shot into a fair one. Most of all, Rearward keeps him away from swords. Note: the wind-proof lantern does not help here, because useful items work only outside combat p.49.

Example: Round 2: Tom rolls Scan, Feat dice 4 and 9 (keeps 9) + 2, 5, 6. Total 22, with an icon: +2d on his next shot. Round 3: he shoots with 3 Success dice and spends 1 Hope for a 4th.

3. An enemy in my face

When: an enemy is fighting Tom up close, and running is not an option yet.

  1. Pick the stance. Open if you want a fair fight. Forward if the enemy must fall fast (+1d to your attack, but +1d to theirs). Defensive if you only need to last until help comes p.96.
  2. Attack with the spear (main action): Feat die + 2 Success dice (3 in Forward), against TN 16 + the enemy's Parry p.98. A hit costs the enemy 4 Endurance p.98.
  3. Spend each icon from a hit on one of these p.99:
    • Pierce: +3 to the Feat die number with a spear. A Feat die of 7 or more becomes a Piercing Blow. The enemy rolls against Injury 14 (16 in two hands).
    • Heavy Blow: the enemy loses 4 more Endurance (your Strength), or 5 with the spear in two hands.
    • Fend Off: +3 to your Parry for this round (18 becomes 21).
  4. Their turn: enemies must reach your Parry 18 to hit you p.98.
Open about 21% vs TN 17Forward about 46%Forward +1 Hope: about 72%

Why: with only 2 dice, Tom misses often in Open. Forward almost doubles his chance, but he gets hit more. Use Forward when a friend is also on the same enemy, or when it is nearly beaten.

One hand or two? The spear works in one or two hands p.48. Two hands give Injury 16 and a bigger Heavy Blow. Our reading: two hands means the buckler is not in use, so Parry drops to 16. The book does not spell this out; ask your Loremaster.

4. I'm getting tired

When: Tom's Endurance is 17 or less. That is his Load, so he is Weary p.22 p.69. Only 7 points of damage take him there.

While Weary, every Success die that shows 1, 2 or 3 counts as 0 p.22. Even his great Stealth drops from about 90% to about 70%.

  1. Take off the helm (a secondary action) p.97 p.48. Load drops from 17 to 13. Weary ends as soon as Endurance is above Load again, so at 14 to 17 Endurance he is fine again p.71. The price: Protection drops from 3d to 2d.
  2. Roll with a big hit (Knockback). Once per round, when a hit lands, you can halve the Endurance you lose (round up). You then spend your next main action getting back into position p.98 p.99.
  3. After the fight, rest. A short rest (about an hour, once a day) gives back 4 Endurance (his Strength). A night's sleep gives back all of it. If Wounded: nothing from a short rest, and only 4 from a night p.71.
helm off: Load 13, Protection 2dKnockback: half damage, lose a main action

Why: Tom's Load is high for his Endurance. The helm is the one piece the book lets him drop in combat. Do it early, before the next hit.

Journeys make this worse. Fatigue from travel raises your Load until you shake it off, so you become Weary sooner p.69 p.111.

5. Badly hit, or time to go

When: the Loremaster says Tom took a Piercing Blow, or a friend is down, or the fight is lost.

  1. Protection roll. Roll the Feat die + 3 Success dice (2 with the helm off), against the Injury of the enemy's weapon, often 14 or 16 p.99 p.150. If it fails, Tom is Wounded.
  2. First Wound: tick the Wounded box and roll a Feat die for how bad it is. The Eye means Tom is Dying p.101. A second Wound drops him to 0 Endurance, unconscious and Dying p.101.
  3. Get out, way A: next round, choose Rearward. On your turn you leave the fight. No roll p.104.
  4. Get out, way B: choose Defensive and attack. If the attack hits, you do no damage, but you get away. If it misses, you stay engaged p.104.
  5. Friend down: carrying a fallen friend to safety is a main action p.97. A Dying hero needs a successful Healing roll within about an hour p.101. Tom has Healing 0, so let a friend with Healing roll.
Protection 3d about 54% vs 163d about 69% vs 14helm off 2d about 27% vs 16

Why: losing Endurance is normal. Only a Piercing Blow can kill p.71. So when an enemy is scoring Piercing Blows, keep the helm on, and think about leaving. Way A is safest, if Rearward is allowed.

6. Hope is running low

When: you are about to spend Hope, or Tom's Hope (max 14) is getting close to his Shadow.

  1. Best value: be Inspired. On a skill roll, name a feature that fits: Inquisitive (spotting a lie, a clue), Rustic (seeing through fancy show), Burglary (locks, pockets, getting in). Then 1 Hope gives +2d p.20 p.67.
  2. Spend where it changes the result. Hope helps most on rolls that are about 50-50. Persuade goes from about 50% to about 76% with Hope, and about 92% Inspired. Awareness (1 die, TN 16) only goes from about 10% to about 27%.
  3. Help a friend. Spend 1 Hope so a friend gains 1d, if Tom has at least 1 rank in a fitting skill. Only one helper per roll. If the friend is Tom's Fellowship Focus (a chosen close friend), they gain 2d p.20 p.21 p.55.
  4. Get Hope back. When resting, the Company can spend Fellowship points for Hope. In the Fellowship phase Tom gets back 4 (his Heart). At 0 Hope, a night's rest gives back 1 p.71.
1 Hope = +1d1 Hope Inspired = +2dShadow at or above Hope = Miserable

Why: when Shadow is equal to or higher than his current Hope, Tom is Miserable: an Eye on the Feat die then fails the roll, whatever the total p.22. Every Hope spent brings that line closer. At 0 Hope, he cannot push himself at all p.71.

7. Sneak, search, slip in

When: no fight yet. Tom wants to go unseen, spot something, or get into a closed place. This is where he shines.

  1. Sneak: Stealth 3, Favoured, against Wits TN 13 about 90% p.65.
  2. Search or keep watch: Scan 3, Favoured, TN 13 about 90%. In the dark, his wind-proof lantern can give +1d if the Loremaster agrees p.49.
  3. Locks, pockets, getting in: the Loremaster says which skill fits. Name Burglary to be Inspired p.45 p.67.
  4. Spend icons on a success: go quietly, make haste, or gain extra information (for example, spot a guard) p.19.
  5. Ambush: every hero rolls Stealth, and all must succeed. Then the enemies get no volley and no actions in round 1 p.95. Spend Hope to support a friend with a low Stealth p.20 p.21.
no Hope needed+1 Hope: about 98%while Weary about 70%

Why: Stealth and Scan are Tom's two best rolls by far. Use them to avoid fights or to start them on your terms.

Being ambushed is different. Then everyone rolls Awareness (Strength TN 16). Tom has Awareness 1 about 10%. If he fails, he gets no volley and no action in round 1 p.95. Taking things from innocent folk can count as theft, a Misdeed worth 3 Shadow p.139.

8. On the road and in the hall

When: the Company sets out on a journey, or meets someone important in a formal talk (a council).

Journey

  1. Take the Scout role. There are four roles: Guide, Hunter, Look-out and Scout p.109. Events test Scouts with Explore, Look-outs with Awareness, and Hunters with Hunting p.112.
  2. As Scout: Explore 1 against Wits TN 13, plus 1d from his homemade maps if the Loremaster agrees p.49. With the maps about 50%; with Hope about 76%.
  3. Avoid Look-out, Hunter and Guide: they roll Awareness, Hunting or Travel, all at TN 16 with rank 1 about 10% p.110 p.112.

Council

  1. Know the goal. The Loremaster sets the Resistance: the number of successes the group needs. It is 3 if the hosts lose nothing by agreeing, 6 if the group gains more than the hosts, and 9 if the ask is risky for the hosts p.104 p.105.
  2. Introduction: one spokesperson rolls. A success gives the group as many tries as the Resistance, plus 1 per icon. A failure gives the same tries, but a failed council becomes a Disaster p.106. Courtesy suits friendly hosts; Riddle suits doubtful ones p.106.
  3. Talk: Persuade 2 or Riddle 2 (TN 13), Courtesy 3 or Insight 2 Favoured (TN 16). Each icon counts as an extra success p.106. Good roleplay on points that matter to the hosts can give +1d or +2d p.107.
Persuade about 50%Courtesy about 54%Insight about 44%Inspired Persuade about 92%

Why: as Scout, Tom's maps and Wits finally pay off on the road. In a council, he is a solid second voice. Let the friendliest speaker introduce, then Tom pushes with Persuade or digs with Riddle.

Example: In the Dunland hall the group asks for news of a masked donkey (Resistance 3). A friend opens with Courtesy and succeeds: 3 tries. Tom says "I'm nosy, I know" and names Inquisitive, spends 1 Hope, and rolls Riddle with 4 dice.
