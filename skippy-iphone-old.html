Background

Who Skippy is: pirate background, the story of The Widow, and Captain Edwine Blackscale.

Skippy

Half-orc Artificer 3 (Artillerist) · Pirate (Sailor variant) · chaotic neutral · 900 XP

Languages: Common, Draconic · darkvision 60 ft · passive Perception 12

Ex powder monkey of The Widow. Searching for Captain Edwine Blackscale, a bronze dragonborn. Companion: Mourning Bell, a Tiny bell-shaped black iron cannon with no legs, fired from the shoulder.

Why "Skippy"? story

The ship's captain was called "the skipper". The captain took him in as a small boy. The crew said "the skipper's boy", then "skipper's", then Skippy. He kept the name because it is the only thing the captain gave him.

Backstory story

Skippy never knew his parents. A half-orc child alone on the docks, he was about to be sent to a work house when the captain of The Widow bought his freedom with a bag of coins and a joke. The captain was a bronze dragonborn named Edwine Blackscale. The crew called her "the skipper".

On the ship, Skippy was a powder monkey: the boy who runs gunpowder from the powder room to the cannons during a fight. Fast, hot, loud and dangerous. He learned every cannon by sound. The captain taught him charts and gadgets, and he learned to cast through his tools. The crew spoke Draconic, so he did too.

Then came the storm and a rival ship's guns. The Widow went down in one night. Skippy woke on a beach with the ship's last cannon, a small bell-shaped black iron gun. Years later he found a way to shrink it with magic. Now it is his Eldritch Cannon, Mourning Bell.

The captain was never found. Skippy does not believe she is dead. He maps every coast he visits and marks where she could have washed ashore.

Why Bad Reputation and chaotic neutral? PHB p.139

Port towns still remember The Widow and a half-orc with a cannon. They are afraid, so he gets away with small crimes. He is loyal to one person, not to rules: he will break any law to find the captain, but he will not break a promise.

Looks story

Age 22 · 6 ft 1 in · 210 lb · amber eyes · gray-green skin · black hair tied back with a rope end. Tall and lean, small tusks when he grins, old powder burns on his hands. Patched sailor's coat over studded leather. Talks to his tools as if they were crew. On his shoulder: Mourning Bell, WIDOW stamped on the barrel.

Captain Edwine Blackscale PHB p.32-34

Bronze dragonborn. Edwine is her own name. Blackscale is her pirate name, in the style of Blackbeard (the real pirate Edward Teach): the soot, smoke and grime of many battles darkened her bronze scales.

  • Breath: a line of lightning (Dex save); resists lightning damage.
  • Look: tall, battle-darkened bronze scales, green-gold eyes, a long scar on the left of her snout, a faded blue captain's coat, a brass spyglass.
  • Personality: booming, generous, a little vain. Terrible jokes. She never left a crew member behind, until the night The Widow sank.

The Widow and Mourning Bell T p.17-18

Notes: Roleplay

How Skippy talks, acts and believes. A guideline, not a rulebook: take what is fun.

Skippy in one look story

A half-orc Artillerist artificer, level 3. A former powder monkey (the boy who runs gunpowder to the cannons) on the pirate ship The Widow. His captain, Edwine Blackscale (a bronze dragonborn), raised him. The ship sank. He is searching for her.

His cannon, Mourning Bell, is a small bell-shaped black iron cannon. It has no legs and is Tiny. He fires it from his shoulder like a rocket launcher, or sets it down as a turret. It tolls for the lost crew. His lucky charm is a small red coral horn on a cord around his neck.

His heart story

Loyal (chaotic neutral). A crew is a family. He will break a law, but never a promise. He keeps his word like treasure. He hums the old songs of The Widow's crew. His flaw: he cannot stop looking at a blank map.

The big idea: Skippy lost his ship, his captain and his crew. The old customs are how he holds on to them. His luck rituals are not silly to him. They are memory. Play them with a straight face and a small smile.

His voice made up

  • Short and clear. A powder monkey learned to speak so people hear him over cannon noise.
  • Quick when he is excited, slow and quiet when he talks about the captain.
  • Clever, and he knows it. A small proud smile when he explains how something works. Then he looks at a map and forgets you.
  • Plain English and a few sea words. One sea word per scene is plenty. The luck is in what he does.

How others see him made up

The frightened: villagers step back, guards watch his hands. This is Bad Reputation at work.

"I only bite on Tuesdays." (Then he checks it is not Tuesday.)

The surprised: people expect a brute. He pulls out a chart and a spyglass.

"Ah, you thought I'd smash it. I'm going to measure it."

  • "Fair winds, shipmate."
  • "Belay that. I've got a better idea."
  • "I gave my word. That's the end of it."
  • "Powder, shot, match. In that order."

The Widow's Articles made up

Six short rules Skippy can quote. Real pirate crews swore to written "articles" (fair shares, no fighting aboard, a vote for big choices).

  • No one is left behind.
  • A promise given is a debt owed.
  • The powder is for the guns, not for fools.
  • The skipper's word is the tide.
  • The bell is rung for every one we lose.
  • A name is not sold.

"Article two. I said I would carry it. So I carry it."

  • "Fire first, talk after. But never lie to a fire."

He breaks his luck rule for the captain: his bond is stronger than his fear.

A quiet moment made up

When a friend or NPC dies, Skippy taps Mourning Bell once with his knuckle and says nothing. One tap per person lost. The best use of the cannon's name.

No whistling on deck real world

Sailors thought whistling could call up a wind, or a storm.

Skippy hums but never whistles. "Stop. You'll wake the wind."

Never rename a ship real world

A named ship was thought to have its own spirit.

"My name is the only thing the skipper gave me. I'll never change it. And you don't rename a boat."

The ship's bell and the dead real world

Bells marked time and were tied to death at sea.

When someone falls, he taps Mourning Bell once. One tap per person lost.

Coins for the drowned real world

A coin under the mast for luck, a folk link to paying the ferryman of the dead.

He presses an old coin from The Widow to the mast. "For the ones who didn't make it to shore."

Davy Jones' locker real world

A pirate name for the bottom of the sea.

"She's in Davy's locker." Then: "No. She isn't. I'd feel it."

Touch iron real world

An old sailors' custom: touch iron, not wood, to push bad luck away. His best one, because Mourning Bell is black iron.

Before he fires: "Touch iron," and two knocks on the barrel. It sounds like a tiny bell.

Salt over the left shoulder real world

Spilled salt: throw a pinch over the left shoulder, at the devil behind you.

The horns sign and the red horn real world

A hand sign (index and little finger up) and a small red horn charm against the evil eye. A charm should be a gift. Never point the horns sign at a person: he makes it toward the ground.

"You don't buy your own. Somebody has to love you enough to give it."

The evil eye real world

The belief that an envious look, or a too-big compliment, brings harm. A folk test: drops of olive oil in a bowl of water. If the oil breaks up, the eye is there.

An NPC praises the cannon: he touches iron and says "Bless it."

Unlucky days, unlucky 17 real world

No wedding and no voyage on Friday or Tuesday. In this custom 17 is the unlucky number, not 13.

His Intelligence is 17: "Seventeen is unlucky. But it is my best number. Don't tell the sea." (The dice always stand.)

Bread never upside down real world

Bad luck to put a loaf crust-side down.

He flips it quietly. "The sea knows."

Saints and painted eyes real world

He prays to Saint Rosalia for the sick and says Saint Francis of Paola's name when he crosses water. Painted eyes on a bow protect a boat.

He chalks two small eyes on Mourning Bell. "So it sees where it's going."

Bad Reputation at the table PHB p.139

In towns, ordinary people are scared of him and let small crimes go. It is a story tool, not a dice roll.

  • Ask the DM: "Does anyone here know The Widow?"
  • Good side: a guard looks away, a shopkeeper stops haggling.
  • Bad side: a bounty hunter or an enemy captain may know the name too. That is a story gift.
  • Not for big theft, murder or arson.

"I lean on the counter and say nothing. The Widow's name does the talking."

People often fear half-orcs before they know them. Skippy never knew his parents. He grew up on a ship with dragonborn, speaks Draconic and has forgotten most of his Orc. He looks like an orc, talks like a dragonborn sailor, and belongs to The Widow.

Dragon gods (ask the DM) not your books

Bahamut (justice) and Tiamat (greed). A pirate captain sits in between.

Keep it fun made up

Do not: stop the fight for a ritual, refuse a group vote, mock a real faith, point the horns sign at people, keep an accent all evening.

Your turn, in one picture PHB p.189-190

On your turn you get movement (30 ft), one action, maybe one bonus action, and you can talk a little. Once per round you also have one reaction, even on other people's turns.

  • Movement can be split: walk, act, walk again.
  • If you do not use the bonus action, nothing happens. For Skippy the bonus action is the cannon.
  • The reaction is for Shield.

The d20 roll PHB p.7

Almost everything is: roll a 20-sided die (d20), add a number, and compare with a target. Attack rolls beat the enemy's AC. Saves and checks beat a DC (difficulty number) the DM or the spell sets.

Advantage and disadvantage PHB p.173

Advantage: roll two d20 and keep the higher. Disadvantage: roll two and keep the lower. They cancel each other out. Faerie Fire gives your friends advantage. Shooting the crossbow next to an enemy gives you disadvantage.

Check, save or attack? PHB p.174-179, p.194

Ability check: you try something (search, climb, scare): d20 + skill. Saving throw: you resist something bad: d20 + save. Attack roll: d20 + attack bonus against AC. Your spells make enemies roll saves against your DC 13.

Rests PHB p.186

Short rest (1 hour): spend hit dice to heal (d8+2 each). Slots do NOT come back. Long rest (8 hours): full HP, all 3 slots, the free cannon, Relentless Endurance, and half your hit dice come back.

Dropping to 0 HP PHB p.197

First, Relentless Endurance keeps you at 1 HP (once per long rest). After that you fall unconscious and roll death saves each turn: 10+ is a success, 3 successes = stable, 3 fails = dead. Any healing wakes you.

Words A to C glossary

AC (Armor Class): how hard you are to hit. Yours is 15.

Action: the main thing you do on your turn.

Attunement: a bond with a magic item; Repeating Shot needs it. Max 3 items.

Bonus action: a small extra thing, only when a rule gives one. Yours: the cannon.

Cantrip: a spell with no slot cost.

Concentration: some spells last only while you focus. One at a time.

Cover: half cover +2 AC, three-quarters +5.

Words D to P glossary

DC: difficulty number to reach on a roll. Your spells: 13.

DM: Dungeon Master, the person who runs the game.

Hit dice: dice you spend on a short rest to heal.

Infusion: an artificer's magic put into a normal item.

Initiative: roll at the start of a fight to set the turn order.

Modifier: the small number from an ability score (Int 17 gives +3).

Proficiency: being trained; add +2.

Words R to Z glossary

Reaction: one quick response per round, even on others' turns.

Ritual: casting a spell 10 minutes slower, with no slot.

Saving throw (save): a roll to resist something.

Slot: fuel for a level-1 spell. You have 3 per long rest.

Temporary HP: extra HP that are lost first. They do not add up.

Turn / round: a round is everyone's turn once, about 6 seconds.

XP: experience points. Skippy has 900 (level 3).

Slot budget

Shield, Thunderwave, Cure Wounds, Faerie Fire and False Life each cost 1 of your 3 slots. A second cannon in one day costs one too. Plan them before the day starts. False Life (1d4+4 temp HP for 1 hour) is best just before a fight you can see coming. T p.17-18, PHB p.239

Concentration

Faerie Fire, Detect Magic and Guidance all use concentration. Casting one ends the other, even the Guidance cantrip. PHB p.203
